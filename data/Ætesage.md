```
Etymology
```
```
It is likely that this farm name comes from “flekk”, Old
Norse “flekkr” (meaning “spot” or “patch”).
Old name forms: Fleke NRJ. Flocke 1563. Flecke 1567. Later Fleche up to
```
1723. In more recent times: Flække and Flekke.


Geographical features

On the steep mountainside toward Stavsheia there is a large light patch that
stands out clearly, with reddish granite against dark hornblende. When one
travels inward along Dalsfjorden where the Flekkefjord branches off to the
southeast, this light patch on the mountainside is easily visible. For
comparison, it can be mentioned that by the farm Kviteblik in Askvoll there is
also such a spot in the mountainside. Other farms around the country have
received their names in a similar way.

It may also be noted that people on the farm have believed the name came
from the shape of the land in Flekke, where there are various distinct patches
of cultivated ground — “flekker” — that were gradually brought under
cultivation.

```
Location and topography
```
```
Flekke lies at the end of Flekkefjorden, which at the head of the fjord divides
into two branches. A northern branch goes in toward the farm itself, and a
southern branch ends toward Fløsjøen, where the waterways from Guddal and
from the “Votne villages” flow out. The farmyards lie from 10 to 44 meters
above sea level.
The land alternates between flat slopes and steep hillsides with terraces that
ice and water have built up along the way toward Stavsdalen east of the farm.
Toward Igelkjøn, Kletten reaches 162 meters above sea level; toward
Rakneberg on the north side of Stavsdalen, Blåfjellet rises to 345 meters above
sea level. In the southeast, Stavsheia (is) 518 meters above sea level; in the
southward direction, Tyrvedalshelleren (is) 488 meters above sea level; Nipa
in the south (is) 305 meters above sea level.
```
```
From Stavsdalen, a side valley goes southeast toward Klibberdalsfjellet and
ends there in a narrow throat. In this valley lies the Flekke summer farm
(Flekke-stølen) at 317 meters above sea level.
```

Property Conditions

Flekke belonged to the Stigtens gods, the large estate attached to the bishop's
see in Bergen. At the Reformation in 1537, the entire estate was taken over by
the king. In 1662, King Frederick III sold the estate to Danish archbishop
Hans Svane, placing Flekke under Svanebirket until 1727, when local farmers
bought their occupied holdings: Finn Knutson (1 pound 3 marks for 64 daler),
Halvor Knudson (18 marks for 42 daler), Ole Olson (1 pound 12 marks for
61 daler), and Anders Larsson (1 pound 3 marks for 64 daler).

Tax Records (1520–1598)

In 1520, two holdings existed in Flekke, subject to a 10% extraordinary silver
tax on all property. Herloff paid 30 lodd of silver, and Asle paid 15 lodd. From
1563–1598, only Kolbein (Kolbenn or Kolbiørn) appears in tax lists; in 1590,
records note "Kolbiørn in Flecke uses a salmon fishery," requiring annual tax.

Residents and Censuses (1598–1701)

From 1598–1624, two farmers: Jørgen and Gregorius. By 1624, three: "The
Widow," Østen, and Find. Bailiff Busch's 1626 cadastre describes Flekke as "a
good land with a fine salmon fishery." From 1627–1638: Østen and Find.

In 1640–1641: Brite (likely Østen's widow), Knud, and Find. The 1645 head
tax lists: Find and wife (1 boy, 2 girls); Jonn and wife (1 girl); "The Widow"
(1 boy, 1 girl).

The 1657 livestock census records "The Widow" and Jonn with 20 cattle, 12
sheep, 12 goats; Knud and "The Widow" with 12 cattle, 11 sheep, 8 goats.
